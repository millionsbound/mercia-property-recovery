Deposit and damage claims after a cannabis farm or tenant damage

You can claim against the tenancy deposit for cleaning, rubbish removal, repairs and lock changes. But the deposit is usually small compared with the damage, and the adjudicator cannot award more than the deposit. Here is how a real case was decided.

A real case from mydeposits

mydeposits, one of the government-approved deposit protection schemes, published a case where a property had been used to grow cannabis. The deposit was £2,650 and the whole amount was disputed.

The evidence: tenancy agreement, check-in report, check-out report, estimates, invoices and emails.

What was decided:

Item Claimed Awarded Why
Lock change (keys not returned) £220 £220 Tenant must return all keys; illegal activity made a full lock change reasonable
Removal of compost, soil, pots, sheeting, freezer, mattress and rubbish £250 £250 None of it was there at check-in
Full clean £320 £288 Reduced slightly for the condition at the start
Repairs: windows, ceiling plasterboard, floorboards, screw holes, doors £2,450 £1,892 Assessed at £1,960 after wear and tear, then capped because the total award cannot exceed the deposit

The landlord received the full deposit. The repairs alone were worth more than that.

What adjudicators look for

  • Check-in and check-out reports that can be compared room by room
  • Photos, dated, of the same areas at both ends of the tenancy
  • Itemised invoices or estimates, not a single lump sum
  • An allowance for wear and tear and the starting condition. In the case above, the cleaning and repair figures were reduced for this
  • Correspondence showing you tried to inspect and the tenant did not allow access

When the damage is more than the deposit

Most cannabis farm restorations cost far more than a typical deposit. Options include:

  1. Your landlord insurance, if it covers malicious damage or drug cultivation and you met the conditions. See the inspection trap guide.
  2. A claim against the tenant through the county court (the online Money Claim service for smaller amounts). This is only worthwhile if the tenant can be traced and has money or assets. Take advice first.
  3. A guarantor, if there was one, depending on the guarantee wording.

Practical tips

  • Do not start repairs until you have photographed everything and your insurer has had a chance to inspect.
  • Ask your contractor for separate line items for clearance, cleaning, electrics, damp and each repair. It makes both the deposit and insurance claim easier.
  • Keep waste transfer notes. They show the rubbish was real and was disposed of properly.

The deposit basics that still apply

In England, a deposit on an assured shorthold tenancy has to be protected in one of the government-approved schemes: Deposit Protection Service (DPS), mydeposits or Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS). GOV.UK sets out the rules. If the deposit was not protected properly, you may struggle to deduct anything and could face a penalty claim, so check the protection certificate and the prescribed information before you start.

At the end of the tenancy you propose deductions. If the tenant disagrees, each scheme offers a free dispute resolution service. If the tenant has disappeared, which is common after a cannabis farm, each scheme has its own process for releasing the deposit when one party does not respond. Read your scheme's rules for the steps and the time limits.

Building an evidence file that wins

Adjudicators decide on paper. They do not visit. Your file should let a stranger see the damage, the starting condition and the cost in ten minutes.

  1. Check-in report, signed or acknowledged, with photos of every room.
  2. Mid-tenancy inspection reports, if you have them. They show when the condition changed.
  3. Police and crime reference details, showing the property was used to grow cannabis.
  4. Check-out evidence: dated photos and video taken before any clear-up, from the same angles as check-in where possible.
  5. Quotes or invoices, itemised. One line per item: removal of waste, cleaning, each repair.
  6. Waste transfer notes from a registered carrier.
  7. A short covering statement: what happened, when you found out, what you are claiming and why.

Wear and tear and betterment

Adjudicators will not let you charge the tenant for making a property better than it was. In the mydeposits case, the repair claim of £2,450 was assessed at £1,960 after allowing for wear and tear, and the cleaning claim was cut from £320 to £288 for the starting condition. Expect the same reasoning on your claim:

  • A carpet that was eight years old at check-in is not worth the price of a new one.
  • Redecoration is usually apportioned by how long ago the rooms were last painted.
  • Damage the tenant clearly caused, such as holes cut for ducting, is easier to recover in full.

Going to court for the rest

If the damage is far more than the deposit and insurance does not cover it, a county court money claim is the remaining route. GOV.UK runs an online money claim service. Before you start:

  • Can you find the tenant? You need an address to serve the claim. If the tenant has been charged, the court case may be public, but that does not give you an address.
  • Can they pay? A judgment against someone with no money or assets costs you fees and gives you a piece of paper.
  • Is there a guarantor? A guarantee may cover damage as well as rent, depending on its wording.
  • What does it cost? Court fees depend on the amount claimed. Check the current fees on GOV.UK.

Possession and the deposit

If you need a possession order because the tenant has not formally left, GOV.UK's guidance says the deposit protection conditions that apply to most possession grounds do not apply to Ground 14, the anti-social behaviour ground that includes illegal use. That is about getting possession, not about deductions. You still need to protect the deposit properly to claim against it. Our first 48 hours guide covers possession.

A worked example (illustrative only)

The numbers below are made up to show the reasoning, not taken from a real case. Say the deposit is £1,200 and you claim:

Item Claimed Likely outcome
Removal of grow waste, with waste transfer note £400 Likely in full: none of it was there at check-in
Lock change, keys not returned £180 Likely in full
Cleaning £350 Possibly reduced for the starting condition
Replastering two ceilings cut for ducting £900 Likely, if photos show the holes and check-in shows they were not there
New carpets throughout £1,600 Reduced for age; an eight-year-old carpet is worth little

Even after reductions, the claim is well above £1,200, so the award is capped at the deposit. The rest has to come from insurance, a guarantor or a court claim.

Order of play

  1. Photograph everything before the clear-up.
  2. Get itemised quotes, then invoices.
  3. Propose deductions to the tenant in writing, with the evidence.
  4. If there is no agreement or no reply, follow your scheme's dispute or single-claim process.
  5. Decide whether the shortfall is worth a court claim.

Questions landlords ask

Can the deposit scheme award more than the deposit?

No. The adjudicator can only award up to the amount of the deposit in dispute. For anything above that, you need another route, such as insurance or a court claim.

Do I need a professional inventory?

It is not legally required, but in disputes a detailed, dated check-in report with photos makes a large difference.

The tenant has vanished. Can I still claim the deposit?

Usually yes. Each scheme has a process for when one party does not respond. Follow your scheme's steps and keep a record of how you tried to contact the tenant.

Can I claim the cost of stolen electricity from the deposit?

If the energy supplier bills you for it, you can include it in a claim against the tenant, but adjudicators will want to see the bill and a clear link to the tenancy. For larger amounts, a court claim may be needed.
