f:T806,<h2>Recent cases</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mattox Street, Wednesfield.</strong> Wolverhampton Crown Court heard in October 2026 that police found 250 to 300 plants across two of three bedrooms and the loft of a house in August. The electricity was being abstracted. The court ordered the hydroponic equipment destroyed.</li>
<li><strong>Peverill Road, Springvale, 6 October 2026.</strong> Around 200 plants were found in a house when officers executed a warrant.</li>
<li>The Express and Star also reported finds in Colliery Road in Eastfields, in Low Hill, and in Riley Street, Willenhall.</li>
</ul>
<p>The pattern is clear: residential houses, multiple rooms and the loft, and electricity theft. That means <strong>electrical make-safe</strong> is almost always the first job.</p>
<h2>What Wolverhampton landlords should know</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lofts.</strong> Several local finds involved the loft. Check loft insulation for damage, mould and needles before anyone works up there, and check the joists where heavy equipment was hung.</li>
<li><strong>Licensing.</strong> Mandatory HMO licensing applies across the city for properties let to five or more people from two or more households. Check the council&#39;s HMO licensing page for any current local schemes before re-letting.</li>
<li><strong>Court dates.</strong> Where there is a prosecution, it is likely to be at Wolverhampton Crown Court. Your insurer may ask for the crime reference number and any outcome. Keep a note.</li>
</ul>
<h2>The first steps</h2>
<ol>
<li>Get the crime reference number and confirm the property has been released.</li>
<li>Call 105 or your supplier about the meter. Do not switch anything on.</li>
<li>Tell your insurer and ask whether they want to send a loss adjuster.</li>
<li>Photograph everything before it is moved.</li>
<li>Then arrange the clean-up, electrics and repairs, in that order.</li>
</ol>
