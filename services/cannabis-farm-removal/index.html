What the police remove, and what they leave behind

West Midlands Police has a Cannabis Disposal Team made up of specialist police staff. On its cannabis disposal page, the force says the team's main job is to dismantle cannabis farms safely by:

  • removing cannabis plants and chemicals
  • collecting evidence from the scene
  • making the building safe
  • destroying the equipment used to grow the plants
  • recycling parts where possible

The plants are burnt at a waste-to-energy plant with an officer watching. That is the police side of the job, and it is done well. But "making the building safe" means safe from the grow itself. It does not mean clean, repaired or fit to let. Once the property is released, this is what landlords usually find is still there:

  • soil, compost and spilt nutrient solution trodden into carpets and floorboards
  • staples, screws, battens and torn sheeting or reflective foil on walls and ceilings
  • holes cut for ducting through ceilings, loft hatches and outside walls
  • tampered wiring and a bypassed meter that still need a proper repair
  • damp, mould, rotten skirting and a sweet, heavy smell in the plaster
  • rubbish, belongings and anything that was not part of the grow itself

Exactly what goes varies from job to job. Ask the officer in the case what has been taken and whether anything is still to be collected before you book anyone. For the police side in more detail, read do the police remove a cannabis farm?

Cannabis factory removal: what the job covers

Firms call it cannabis factory removal, grow house clean up or grow room clearance. The name changes; the job does not. A typical clear-up after a grow includes:

  1. Strip-out of sheeting, foil, battens, fixings and any ducting the police left in place.
  2. Waste removal by a registered waste carrier, with a waste transfer note for each load. Lamps, ballasts and fans count as electrical waste and have their own rules.
  3. Contaminated soft furnishings and carpets taken out and disposed of.
  4. Decontamination and a deep clean of hard surfaces, with mould treatment where it is needed.
  5. Odour treatment. Cleaning alone rarely shifts the smell from plaster and timber, so specialists use sealing primers or other treatments.
  6. A short written report: what was found, what was removed, where the waste went and what still needs doing.

Small grows still leave a lot. In the mydeposits case study we cite in our deposit guide, compost, soil, plastic pots and sheeting were left behind, and the invoice to remove them and other rubbish was £250. Bigger grows leave a great deal more.

How cannabis farm removal works, step by step

This is the order a good job follows. It keeps people safe and keeps your insurance claim intact.

1. Call-back and the basics

Hamza calls you back and asks for the crime reference number, whether the police have released the property, whether you have told your insurer and whether the power is off. If the property is still a crime scene, nobody goes in, and we line someone up for when it is released.

2. Survey and photos before anything moves

The specialist visits or does a video survey, photographs every room, the loft and the meter cupboard, and then quotes room by room. Those photos are your evidence. They cannot be taken again once the clear-out starts.

3. Strip-out and licensed waste removal

Sheeting, fixings, soil and the remaining equipment come out and go to a licensed site with waste transfer notes. If a firm cannot give you a waste carrier registration number, do not use them. The section below shows how to check one in two minutes.

4. Make the electrics safe

Do not switch anything on. Call 105 about the meter and the supply. A registered electrician then isolates, tests and makes safe the installation and issues a report. If the cable was tapped before the meter, the network operator may need to repair the supply as well. In one Yardley case reported by Birmingham Live, that part alone cost nearly £3,000. See electrical make-safe and rewire.

5. Decontamination, odour treatment and a handover pack

The property is cleaned, treated for mould and odour, and handed back with a pack: before and after photos, the written scope, itemised invoices, waste transfer notes and the electrical report. That is what your insurer, loss adjuster and deposit scheme will ask for.

What it does not cover

Removal and clean-up get the property safe to work in. They do not fix holes, rotten floorboards, damp or the wiring itself. That is cannabis farm damage repair, and a registered electrician for the electrics. If you want one firm to handle all of it, say so and we will look for a specialist who can.

Flats, HMOs and empty commercial units

Not every grow is in a terraced house. The job changes with the building:

  • Flats and maisonettes. Waste comes out through shared stairs and hallways, and holes in ceilings or party walls can affect the flat next door. Tell the freeholder or managing agent before work starts, and ask the specialist how they will protect communal areas.
  • HMOs. If the property is licensed, the repairs have to bring it back to licence standard before anyone moves in: fire doors that close, working alarms, safe electrics and no serious damp. Growers often take doors off and alarms down, so put them on the list.
  • Empty shops, offices and industrial units. Larger grows mean more waste, more electrical equipment and sometimes structural alterations. A grow room clearance in a unit like the Midland Road, Walsall find needs a firm with the vehicles and staff to match.

Say what kind of property it is when you fill in the form, and we will look for a specialist who has done that kind of job before.

How to check a waste carrier licence

Anyone who carries waste for you as a business must be registered with the Environment Agency. If an unregistered firm fly-tips your waste, the waste duty of care means it can come back to you. Checking takes two minutes:

  1. Ask for the registration number. In England, upper tier registrations start with CBDU. Firms carrying other people's waste as a business need upper tier.
  2. Search it on the Environment Agency public register. The name and address should match the firm on your quote.
  3. Check it is current. Upper tier registrations have an expiry date, shown on the register.
  4. Get a waste transfer note for each load, and keep the notes with your claim file.

Before we introduce any firm that will remove waste, we check its registration on the register ourselves, and we give you the number so you can check it too. Our vetting page lists every other check.

Before you book anyone

  • Wait until the police have released the property. If it is still a crime scene, nobody should be inside.
  • Do not switch the electricity back on. Bypassed meters and modified wiring are common.
  • Tell your insurer first if you plan to claim. Some policies want to approve contractors or send a loss adjuster before work starts. Our insurance guide explains the conditions that catch landlords out.

Questions landlords ask

Do the police remove the cannabis farm?

Partly. In the West Midlands the Cannabis Disposal Team removes plants and chemicals, collects evidence, makes the building safe and destroys the growing equipment. Cleaning, waste left behind, electrics, damp and repairs are left for the owner.

Can I just clear it myself or use a normal rubbish removal firm?

You can, but there are risks. Mould, sharp fixings and modified electrics are common, and the waste has to go with a registered carrier to a licensed site with a waste transfer note. If you plan to claim on insurance or against the deposit, a professional report and receipts make that much easier.

How long does cannabis farm removal take?

It depends on how many rooms were used and how long the grow ran. The strip-out and clean for a small grow is often a matter of days, while electrics, drying out and repairs take longer. The specialist will give you a timescale with the written quote.

Does landlord insurance pay for the clean-up?

Some policies do, some exclude it, and many attach conditions such as regular inspections and tenant referencing. Tell your insurer before work starts and ask in writing what they need.

How soon can someone attend?

That depends on the specialist covering your postcode. When we call you back we will tell you who is available and when, before we pass your details on.

Do I pay you?

No. We are an introduction service. You agree the price and pay the specialist directly. We may receive a fee from them, which is explained on our About page.
