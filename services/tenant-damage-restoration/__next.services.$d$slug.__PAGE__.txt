<h2>Abandoned belongings: be careful</h2>
<p>If a tenant has left items behind, you cannot simply bin them straight away. Write to the former tenant at their last known address and any forwarding address, give them a reasonable deadline to collect, and keep a record. Take legal advice if the items look valuable. This is separate from rubbish and obviously worthless items.</p>
<h2>Get the evidence right first</h2>
<p>Deposit adjudicators and insurers decide claims on evidence. Before any work starts:</p>
<ol>
<li>Compare the property against the <strong>check-in inventory</strong> room by room.</li>
<li>Take <strong>dated photos and video</strong> of every room and every defect.</li>
<li>Get <strong>itemised quotes or invoices</strong> for each repair.</li>
<li>Keep copies of <strong>emails and letters</strong> with the tenant.</li>
</ol>
<p>The mydeposits case study we cite shows how this works in practice: each item was judged against the check-in and check-out reports, and the award was limited to the amount of the deposit. Our <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/guides/deposit-and-damage-claims/">deposit guide</a> explains what to do if the damage costs more than the deposit.</p>
