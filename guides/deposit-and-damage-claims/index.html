Deposit and damage claims after a cannabis farm or tenant damage

You can claim against the tenancy deposit for cleaning, rubbish removal, repairs and lock changes. But the deposit is usually small compared with the damage, and the adjudicator cannot award more than the deposit. Here is how a real case was decided.

A real case from mydeposits

mydeposits, one of the government-approved deposit protection schemes, published a case where a property had been used to grow cannabis. The deposit was £2,650 and the whole amount was disputed.

The evidence: tenancy agreement, check-in report, check-out report, estimates, invoices and emails.

What was decided:

Item Claimed Awarded Why
Lock change (keys not returned) £220 £220 Tenant must return all keys; illegal activity made a full lock change reasonable
Removal of compost, soil, pots, sheeting, freezer, mattress and rubbish £250 £250 None of it was there at check-in
Full clean £320 £288 Reduced slightly for the condition at the start
Repairs: windows, ceiling plasterboard, floorboards, screw holes, doors £2,450 £1,892 Assessed at £1,960 after wear and tear, then capped because the total award cannot exceed the deposit

The landlord received the full deposit. The repairs alone were worth more than that.

What adjudicators look for

  • Check-in and check-out reports that can be compared room by room
  • Photos, dated, of the same areas at both ends of the tenancy
  • Itemised invoices or estimates, not a single lump sum
  • An allowance for wear and tear and the starting condition. In the case above, the cleaning and repair figures were reduced for this
  • Correspondence showing you tried to inspect and the tenant did not allow access

When the damage is more than the deposit

Most cannabis farm restorations cost far more than a typical deposit. Options include:

  1. Your landlord insurance, if it covers malicious damage or drug cultivation and you met the conditions. See the inspection trap guide.
  2. A claim against the tenant through the county court (the online Money Claim service for smaller amounts). This is only worthwhile if the tenant can be traced and has money or assets. Take advice first.
  3. A guarantor, if there was one, depending on the guarantee wording.

Practical tips

  • Do not start repairs until you have photographed everything and your insurer has had a chance to inspect.
  • Ask your contractor for separate line items for clearance, cleaning, electrics, damp and each repair. It makes both the deposit and insurance claim easier.
  • Keep waste transfer notes. They show the rubbish was real and was disposed of properly.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

Common questions

Can the deposit scheme award more than the deposit?

No. The adjudicator can only award up to the amount of the deposit in dispute. For anything above that, you need another route, such as insurance or a court claim.

Do I need a professional inventory?

It is not legally required, but in disputes a detailed, dated check-in report with photos makes a large difference.
