22:T68a,<h2>When to call a specialist</h2>
<ul>
<li>You have found, or suspect, used needles anywhere in the property</li>
<li>There are bodily fluids, blood or heavy soiling</li>
<li>The property has been occupied by squatters or used by people other than the tenant</li>
<li>You are a letting agent and need to make a void property safe for staff, inventory clerks and trades</li>
</ul>
<h2>What the job usually involves</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>A sharps sweep</strong> of every room, including soft furnishings, under flooring and in the loft, using puncture-resistant gloves and litter pickers.</li>
<li><strong>Sharps placed in approved containers</strong> and taken away for incineration as clinical waste, with paperwork.</li>
<li><strong>Biohazard cleaning</strong> of contaminated surfaces with appropriate disinfectants.</li>
<li><strong>Removal of contaminated items</strong> such as mattresses and carpets.</li>
<li><strong>A written record</strong> of what was found and removed, which is useful for your insurer, your deposit claim and your own records.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Keep your staff safe</h2>
<p>If you or your staff find a needle, do not try to recap it, do not put it in a normal bin, and do not reach into places you cannot see. Leave it, close the room if you can, and call a specialist. If someone is injured by a needle, they should wash the wound under running water, let it bleed, and get urgent medical advice the same day.</p>
<h2>Rough cost</h2>
<p>One West Midlands specialist publishes needle sweeps from <strong>£250</strong>. Larger clean-ups depend heavily on the size of the property and what is found, so expect a site visit before a firm price.</p>
