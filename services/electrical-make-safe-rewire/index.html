Electrical make-safe, testing and rewire

Electricity theft is a feature of most cannabis farms. The meter may be bypassed, cables may be run straight from the supply, and new circuits may have been added with no protection. This is the most dangerous part of the property. Nobody should switch anything back on until it has been checked.

First: who is responsible for what

  • The meter, the main fuse (cut-out) and the cable coming into the property belong to the energy supplier and the local distribution network, not to you. In most of the West Midlands that network is National Grid Electricity Distribution. If you suspect tampering, call 105, the free national number for power cuts and electrical safety, and tell your energy supplier. In the 973-plant Fleet Street case in Birmingham, reported by the BBC, the supply was made safe by the network operator.
  • Everything after the meter, the consumer unit and the wiring in the property, is the landlord's responsibility. That work must be done by a qualified electrician.

What the electrician will do

  1. Isolate and make safe any unsafe circuits or connections.
  2. Remove grow-room wiring, timers, extra sockets and anything that was not part of the original installation.
  3. Inspect and test the installation and produce an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) listing what needs fixing.
  4. Carry out remedial work, from a replacement consumer unit to a partial or full rewire.
  5. Certify the work. Notifiable work in England must comply with Part P of the Building Regulations, usually by using an electrician registered with a competent person scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT.

Why landlords cannot skip this

Private landlords in England must have the electrical installation inspected and tested at least every five years under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, and must carry out remedial work identified in the report. After a cannabis farm, the old EICR is worthless. You will need a new one before re-letting.

Rough cost

One published estimate puts an electrical rewire and fuse board after a cannabis farm at £2,500 to £5,000. A make-safe and test on its own costs less. The electrician will price the remedial work after the EICR. See the cost guide.

Questions landlords ask

Can my usual electrician do this?

Yes, if they are qualified and registered with a competent person scheme. Tell them it was a cannabis farm so they come prepared for bypassed meters and hidden connections.

Will I be charged for the stolen electricity?

Energy suppliers handle electricity theft cases themselves. Speak to your supplier and keep your crime reference number to hand. Do not agree to anything you do not understand; ask for it in writing.

Is it safe to go in with the power off?

Only once the police have released the property and someone competent has confirmed the supply is isolated. Bypassed meters can mean live cables even when the consumer unit is switched off.
