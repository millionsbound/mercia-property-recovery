22:Ta5e,<h2>Recent cases in the city</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fleet Street, Jewellery Quarter (2023).</strong> The BBC reported 973 plants growing across two floors of a building on Fleet Street in the Jewellery Quarter. The electricity supply had to be made safe by the network operator before the clear-up could begin.</li>
<li><strong>Hockley and Selly Oak, early 2026.</strong> Birmingham Mail reported cannabis farms worth around £700,000 found in Hockley and Selly Oak, including one in what it described as a quiet residential street.</li>
</ul>
<p>Large finds make the news. Smaller grows, in two or three rooms of an ordinary house or flat, rarely do, but they still leave tampered electrics, damp and damage to put right.</p>
<h2>Selective licensing: why it matters after a cannabis farm</h2>
<p>Since 5 June 2023, privately rented single-household homes in <strong>25 of Birmingham&#39;s 69 wards</strong> need a selective licence. The scheme runs until 4 June 2028. The wards include Aston, Handsworth, Lozells, Small Heath, Sparkhill, Bordesley Green, Ladywood, Edgbaston, Bournbrook and Selly Park, Soho and Jewellery Quarter, Stockland Green and others. The council has an address checker. Since the same date, smaller HMOs (three or four people from two or more households) across the whole city also need an additional HMO licence.</p>
<p>What this means in practice:</p>
<ul>
<li>The property must meet licence conditions before it is re-let, so the electrics, damp and repairs need to be done properly and documented.</li>
<li>Keep the new EICR, the waste transfer notes and the invoices. If the council asks, you can show what was done.</li>
<li>If the property was unlicensed when the farm was found, take advice. Penalties for letting without a licence can now reach £40,000.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Practical points for Birmingham</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Student areas</strong> such as Selly Oak and Bournbrook have a high turnover of tenants and many HMOs. Mid-term inspections are often skipped in summer voids, which is when properties are at risk.</li>
<li><strong>Terraced housing</strong> across inner-city wards often has rear access, which helps contractors remove waste without disturbing neighbours.</li>
<li><strong>Parking and access</strong> in the city centre and Jewellery Quarter can affect skip permits and waste collection. Ask the specialist how they will handle it.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How we help in Birmingham</h2>
<p>Tell us the postcode and what you found. We will call you back, check the details, and introduce you to up to two specialists who cover that part of the city. You agree the work and the price with them directly.</p>
