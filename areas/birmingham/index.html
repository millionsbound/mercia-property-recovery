Cannabis farm clean-up and recovery in Birmingham

Many Birmingham finds follow the same pattern: a neighbour reports a smell or a light that never goes off, West Midlands Police force the door, and a day or two later the landlord is handed back a house in Erdington, Small Heath or Selly Oak that nobody wants to walk into.

Birmingham has some of the largest reported cannabis farm finds in the region, and a licensing regime that makes recovery more complicated than elsewhere. This page covers what Birmingham landlords and agents need to know.

Recent cases in the city

  • Fleet Street, Jewellery Quarter (2023). The BBC reported 973 plants growing across two floors of a building on Fleet Street in the Jewellery Quarter. The electricity supply had to be made safe by the network operator before the clear-up could begin.
  • Hockley and Selly Oak, early 2026. Birmingham Mail reported cannabis farms worth around £700,000 found in Hockley and Selly Oak, including one in what it described as a quiet residential street.

Large finds make the news. Smaller grows, in two or three rooms of an ordinary house or flat, rarely do, but they still leave tampered electrics, damp and damage to put right.

Selective licensing: why it matters after a cannabis farm

Since 5 June 2023, privately rented single-household homes in 25 of Birmingham's 69 wards need a selective licence. The scheme runs until 4 June 2028. The wards include Aston, Handsworth, Lozells, Small Heath, Sparkhill, Bordesley Green, Ladywood, Edgbaston, Bournbrook and Selly Park, Soho and Jewellery Quarter, Stockland Green and others. The council has an address checker. Since the same date, smaller HMOs (three or four people from two or more households) across the whole city also need an additional HMO licence.

What this means in practice:

  • The property must meet licence conditions before it is re-let, so the electrics, damp and repairs need to be done properly and documented.
  • Keep the new EICR, the waste transfer notes and the invoices. If the council asks, you can show what was done.
  • If the property was unlicensed when the farm was found, take advice. Penalties for letting without a licence can now reach £40,000.

Practical points for Birmingham

  • Student areas such as Selly Oak and Bournbrook have a high turnover of tenants and many HMOs. Mid-term inspections are often skipped in summer voids, which is when properties are at risk.
  • Terraced housing across inner-city wards often has rear access, which helps contractors remove waste without disturbing neighbours.
  • Parking and access in the city centre and Jewellery Quarter can affect skip permits and waste collection. Ask the specialist how they will handle it.

Which council is it really?

A B postcode does not always mean Birmingham City Council. Parts of B64 to B71 are Sandwell, B90 to B94 are mostly Solihull, and some B postcodes on the northern edge are Walsall. The council matters because licensing, environmental health and building control all sit with the borough the house is actually in. Check the council tax bill or the council's own address checker before you assume.

The shared entry problem

A lot of Birmingham's older terraces have a shared entry: the covered passage between two houses that leads to the back gardens. It is the easiest way to get waste out without carrying it through the front room, but it is shared. If the house next door has a right of way through it, do not block it with a skip or bags, and tell the neighbour when the clear-out is happening. Ask any firm you speak to how they plan to get waste out of the property, and whether they need a skip permit for the road. Birmingham City Council issues skip permits for skips on the highway, and the skip company usually applies.

Re-letting under selective licensing

If the house is in one of the 25 selective licensing wards, think about the licence before you think about decorating:

  1. Tell the licensing team if the licence conditions require you to report a change in circumstances or management. Read your licence document.
  2. Get a new EICR once the electrics are put right. A cannabis farm almost always means the old one is worthless.
  3. Keep the paperwork in one place: waste transfer notes, the EICR, the gas safety record, and invoices for damp and repair work.
  4. Do not re-let early. A house that fails a licence inspection after a cannabis farm is harder to explain than one that sat empty for an extra fortnight.

Student areas and summer voids

Selly Oak, Bournbrook and parts of Harborne have a high share of HMOs and short tenancies. Grows have been found in student areas, including the Selly Oak raid reported by the Birmingham Mail, and summer is when houses can sit with little oversight. If you let in these areas, a mid-summer inspection is cheap insurance, and some landlord policies require inspections at fixed intervals whether the house is full or not. Who to contact in Birmingham

  • West Midlands Police: 101 for non-emergencies, or 999 if a crime is happening. Ask for the crime reference number and the officer in the case.
  • Power and the meter: call 105, the free national electricity safety line. In the West Midlands the network operator is National Grid Electricity Distribution.
  • Energy theft, anonymously: Stay Energy Safe, run by Crimestoppers.
  • Licensing questions: Birmingham City Council's private rented sector team (linked below).

Questions landlords ask

Does Birmingham City Council clear cannabis farms?

No. The police remove plants and some equipment. The council does not clear private property. The owner arranges and pays for clearance, electrics and repairs, which may be covered by landlord insurance depending on the policy.

My Birmingham rental is in a selective licensing ward. Can I re-let straight after the clean-up?

Only once the property meets the licence conditions. Get the electrics made safe and tested, deal with damp and damage, and keep the paperwork. Read your licence for any duty to notify the council of changes.

Do you cover Sutton Coldfield and Solihull?

Sutton Coldfield is part of Birmingham, so yes. For Solihull postcodes, tell us the postcode and we will check whether a vetted specialist covers it before passing your details on.
