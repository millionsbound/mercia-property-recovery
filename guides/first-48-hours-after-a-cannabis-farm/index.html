Police found a cannabis farm in my rental. What do I do in the first 48 hours?

The police have been, the plants have gone, and you are left with a damaged property, a lot of questions and possibly an insurance claim. These are the steps, in order. Most mistakes landlords make in this situation happen in the first two days.

1. Get the crime reference number and the release

Ask the officer in the case for the crime reference number and confirm when the property is released. Until it is, it may still be a crime scene, and you should not go in or let anyone else in. Write down the officer's name and how to contact them. Your insurer will ask for the reference.

2. Do not switch anything on

Cannabis farms usually involve stolen electricity. The meter may be bypassed and cables may be connected directly to the supply. Even with the consumer unit off, parts of the wiring can be live.

  • Call 105, the free national power cut and electrical safety number, or your energy supplier, and tell them the property was a cannabis farm.
  • Do not touch the meter, the main fuse or any cables near them. They belong to the supplier and the network.
  • Arrange a qualified electrician to make safe and test the installation. See electrical make-safe.

3. Tell your insurer, today if you can

Most policies require you to report a claim promptly. Read the section on malicious damage or "illegal cultivation of drugs" and check:

  • whether drug-related damage is covered at all
  • any conditions, such as how often you must inspect the property and keep records (see the inspection trap guide)
  • whether the insurer wants to send a loss adjuster or approve contractors before work starts

Ask the insurer to confirm in writing what they want you to do next.

4. Record everything before anything moves

Once the property is released and it is safe to enter:

  • take dated photos and video of every room, including ceilings, lofts, meter cupboard and outside walls
  • photograph any holes, ducting, sheeting, damp and mould
  • keep anything left behind until your insurer says it can go, unless it is dangerous
  • write a short note of what you saw and when

This is the evidence for your insurer, your deposit claim and any claim against the tenant. It cannot be recreated once the clean-up starts.

5. Check who has possession

This is where landlords get caught out.

  • If the tenant has clearly abandoned the property and returned or left the keys, you can usually secure it. Take advice if you are unsure.
  • If the tenancy has not ended, the tenant still has rights, even if they are under arrest. Changing the locks or removing their belongings without a court order can be unlawful eviction under the Protection from Eviction Act 1977. Using a property for illegal purposes is a ground for possession (Ground 14, Schedule 2, Housing Act 1988), but you still need to follow the court process. Speak to a solicitor.
  • If people other than the tenant were living there, tell the police and take advice.

6. Secure the property

Once you have lawful possession: change the locks, close up holes in external walls, and board windows if needed. Empty, damaged properties attract break-ins and, sometimes, the same people coming back for equipment. Keep the invoice for the lock change. In the mydeposits case we cite, a £220 lock change was awarded in full against the deposit.

7. Tell the people who need to know

  • Your mortgage lender, if your mortgage conditions require it
  • The council's licensing team, if the property is licensed (for example under Birmingham's selective licensing or an HMO licence) and the licence holder must report changes
  • Your letting agent or managing agent, if you have one
  • The deposit scheme, when you are ready to make a claim

8. Get the work scoped in the right order

The order is: make safe the electrics, clear and decontaminate, dry out, repair, decorate, certify. Get a written scope of works and itemised quotes. If you are claiming, send the scope to your insurer before agreeing larger repairs.

9. Keep a file

One folder, paper or digital, with: crime reference, insurer correspondence, photos, quotes, invoices, waste transfer notes, the new EICR and any council correspondence. If the claim is disputed later, this file is what wins it.

Common questions

Can I go into the property before the police release it?

No. Wait for the officer in the case to confirm it has been released. If you are not sure, ask them directly and note the answer.

Will I be prosecuted because a cannabis farm was in my property?

Landlords who did not know are not normally treated as offenders. Allowing premises to be used for producing cannabis knowingly is an offence, so cooperate with the police, keep records of your inspections and checks, and take legal advice if you are worried.

Should I evict the tenant straight away?

Not without following the legal process. Take advice from a solicitor or a landlord association before you change locks or remove belongings.
