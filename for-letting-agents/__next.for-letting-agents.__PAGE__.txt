d:T843,<h2>How it works for agents</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>You or your landlord call us or use the form.</strong> Give us the postcode, what was found and whether the police have released the property.</li>
<li><strong>We call back and qualify the job</strong> so the specialist knows what they are walking into: electrics, waste, damp, access, insurer involvement.</li>
<li><strong>We introduce up to two vetted specialists</strong> who cover the postcode. Your landlord chooses, agrees the price and pays them directly.</li>
<li><strong>You get a copy of the paperwork</strong> if your landlord agrees: photos, scope of works, waste transfer notes, EICR and invoices. That makes the insurance and deposit side easier for everyone.</li>
</ol>
<h2>What we check before we introduce anyone</h2>
<p>See <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/how-we-vet-specialists/">how we vet specialists</a>. In short: company details, waste carrier registration, insurance certificates, electrical registration where relevant, and references.</p>
<h2>Prevention: the inspection condition</h2>
<p>A published Financial Ombudsman decision (DRN-6210910) upheld an insurer&#39;s refusal of a cannabis farm claim because inspections had not been carried out every three months, as the policy required. If your management agreement says six-monthly inspections but your landlords&#39; policies say three-monthly, that gap is a risk for them and a conversation for you. Our <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/guides/landlord-insurance-inspection-trap/">inspection trap guide</a> sets out the decision in plain English. You are welcome to link to it or share it with landlords.</p>
<h2>Referral arrangements</h2>
<p>We are happy to discuss a referral arrangement with agents. If one is agreed, it will be in writing, and you will need to tell your landlords about it, as we do on our <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/about/">About page</a>. We do not pay for introductions that are not disclosed.</p>
