f:Td5a,<h2>What usually gets left behind</h2>
<p>Every property is different, but a typical grow leaves a mix of the following:</p>
<ul>
<li>Compost, soil and spilt nutrient solution, often trodden into carpets and floorboards</li>
<li>Plastic pots, trays, sheeting and reflective foil stapled or screwed to walls and ceilings</li>
<li>Lamps, ballasts, timers, fans and extractor units, which count as electrical waste</li>
<li>Flexible ducting run through holes cut in ceilings, loft hatches and external walls</li>
<li>Windows sealed or boarded from the inside</li>
<li>Heavy condensation, mould and a strong, lingering odour</li>
</ul>
<p>The mydeposits case study we cite in our <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/guides/deposit-and-damage-claims/">deposit guide</a> gives a sense of the small items: compost, soil, plastic pots and sheeting were left in that property, and the invoice to remove them and other rubbish was £250. Bigger grows leave a great deal more.</p>
<h2>What a proper clean-up includes</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>A site visit or video survey</strong> before any price is agreed, so the quote reflects what is actually there.</li>
<li><strong>Photos and video before anything is touched.</strong> Your insurer, deposit scheme or solicitor may need them.</li>
<li><strong>Removal by a registered waste carrier</strong>, with waste transfer notes. Electrical items and some chemicals have their own rules. If a firm cannot give you a waste carrier number, do not use them.</li>
<li><strong>Stripping out</strong> sheeting, foil, fixings, ducting and contaminated soft furnishings.</li>
<li><strong>Decontamination and deep clean</strong> of hard surfaces, with mould treatment where it is needed.</li>
<li><strong>Odour treatment.</strong> Cleaning alone rarely shifts it from plaster and timber. Specialists use sealing primers or other treatments.</li>
<li><strong>A short written report</strong>: what was found, what was removed, where the waste went, and what still needs doing (usually electrics, damp and repairs).</li>
</ol>
<h2>What it does not cover</h2>
<p>A clean-up gets the property safe to work in. It does not fix the electrics, holes, rotten skirting or damp. Those are covered on our <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/services/property-restoration-after-cannabis-farm/">full restoration</a> and <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/services/electrical-make-safe-rewire/">electrical make-safe</a> pages. If you want one firm to handle all of it, say so on the form and we will look for a specialist who can.</p>
<h2>Before you book anyone</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Wait until the police have released the property.</strong> If it is still a crime scene, nobody should be inside.</li>
<li><strong>Do not switch the electricity back on.</strong> Bypassed meters and modified wiring are common. See <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/services/electrical-make-safe-rewire/">electrical make-safe</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Tell your insurer first</strong> if you intend to claim. Some policies want to approve contractors or send a loss adjuster before work starts.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Rough cost</h2>
<p>Published prices for the clean-up stage start at about £900 plus VAT for a straightforward job, and from about £2,000 plus VAT for extensive work. Our <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/guides/cannabis-farm-clean-up-cost/">cost guide</a> shows where those figures come from and what pushes them up.</p>
