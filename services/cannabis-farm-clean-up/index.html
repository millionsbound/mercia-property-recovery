Cannabis farm clean-up for rented property

Police take the plants and some of the equipment. They do not clear the house. What is left is your problem: soil, pots, foil, polythene, lamps, fans, ducting, and a smell that gets into plaster and carpets. We introduce you to a specialist who will clear it lawfully and leave you with the paperwork your insurer and deposit scheme will ask for.

What usually gets left behind

Every property is different, but a typical grow leaves a mix of the following:

  • Compost, soil and spilt nutrient solution, often trodden into carpets and floorboards
  • Plastic pots, trays, sheeting and reflective foil stapled or screwed to walls and ceilings
  • Lamps, ballasts, timers, fans and extractor units, which count as electrical waste
  • Flexible ducting run through holes cut in ceilings, loft hatches and external walls
  • Windows sealed or boarded from the inside
  • Heavy condensation, mould and a strong, lingering odour

The mydeposits case study we cite in our deposit guide gives a sense of the small items: compost, soil, plastic pots and sheeting were left in that property, and the invoice to remove them and other rubbish was £250. Bigger grows leave a great deal more.

What a proper clean-up includes

  1. A site visit or video survey before any price is agreed, so the quote reflects what is actually there.
  2. Photos and video before anything is touched. Your insurer, deposit scheme or solicitor may need them.
  3. Removal by a registered waste carrier, with waste transfer notes. Electrical items and some chemicals have their own rules. If a firm cannot give you a waste carrier number, do not use them.
  4. Stripping out sheeting, foil, fixings, ducting and contaminated soft furnishings.
  5. Decontamination and deep clean of hard surfaces, with mould treatment where it is needed.
  6. Odour treatment. Cleaning alone rarely shifts it from plaster and timber. Specialists use sealing primers or other treatments.
  7. A short written report: what was found, what was removed, where the waste went, and what still needs doing (usually electrics, damp and repairs).

What it does not cover

A clean-up gets the property safe to work in. It does not fix the electrics, holes, rotten skirting or damp. Those are covered on our full restoration and electrical make-safe pages. If you want one firm to handle all of it, say so on the form and we will look for a specialist who can.

Before you book anyone

  • Wait until the police have released the property. If it is still a crime scene, nobody should be inside.
  • Do not switch the electricity back on. Bypassed meters and modified wiring are common. See electrical make-safe.
  • Tell your insurer first if you intend to claim. Some policies want to approve contractors or send a loss adjuster before work starts.

Rough cost

Published prices for the clean-up stage start at about £900 plus VAT for a straightforward job, and from about £2,000 plus VAT for extensive work. Our cost guide shows where those figures come from and what pushes them up.

Questions landlords ask

Can I just clear it myself or use a normal rubbish removal firm?

You can, but there are risks. Mould, sharp fixings and modified electrics are common, and the waste has to go to a licensed site with a waste transfer note. If you plan to claim on insurance or against the deposit, a professional report and receipts make that much easier.

How soon can someone attend?

That depends on the specialist covering your area. When we call you back we will tell you who is available and when, before we pass your details on.

Will the specialist deal with the police or my insurer?

They will not deal with the police on your behalf, but a good firm will give you photos, a written scope and itemised invoices in the format insurers and loss adjusters expect.

Do I pay you?

No. We are an introduction service. You agree the price and pay the specialist directly. We may receive a fee from them, which is explained on our About page.
