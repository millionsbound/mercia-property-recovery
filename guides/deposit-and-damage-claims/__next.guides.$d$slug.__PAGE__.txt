f:Tc4f,<h2>A real case from mydeposits</h2>
<p>mydeposits, one of the government-approved deposit protection schemes, published a case where a property had been used to grow cannabis. The deposit was <strong>£2,650</strong> and the whole amount was disputed.</p>
<p><strong>The evidence:</strong> tenancy agreement, check-in report, check-out report, estimates, invoices and emails.</p>
<p><strong>What was decided:</strong></p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Item</th>
<th>Claimed</th>
<th>Awarded</th>
<th>Why</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Lock change (keys not returned)</td>
<td>£220</td>
<td>£220</td>
<td>Tenant must return all keys; illegal activity made a full lock change reasonable</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Removal of compost, soil, pots, sheeting, freezer, mattress and rubbish</td>
<td>£250</td>
<td>£250</td>
<td>None of it was there at check-in</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Full clean</td>
<td>£320</td>
<td>£288</td>
<td>Reduced slightly for the condition at the start</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Repairs: windows, ceiling plasterboard, floorboards, screw holes, doors</td>
<td>£2,450</td>
<td>£1,892</td>
<td>Assessed at £1,960 after wear and tear, then capped because the total award cannot exceed the deposit</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<p>The landlord received the full deposit. The repairs alone were worth more than that.</p>
<h2>What adjudicators look for</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Check-in and check-out reports</strong> that can be compared room by room</li>
<li><strong>Photos</strong>, dated, of the same areas at both ends of the tenancy</li>
<li><strong>Itemised invoices or estimates</strong>, not a single lump sum</li>
<li><strong>An allowance for wear and tear</strong> and the starting condition. In the case above, the cleaning and repair figures were reduced for this</li>
<li><strong>Correspondence</strong> showing you tried to inspect and the tenant did not allow access</li>
</ul>
<h2>When the damage is more than the deposit</h2>
<p>Most cannabis farm restorations cost far more than a typical deposit. Options include:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Your landlord insurance</strong>, if it covers malicious damage or drug cultivation and you met the conditions. See the <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/guides/landlord-insurance-inspection-trap/">inspection trap guide</a>.</li>
<li><strong>A claim against the tenant</strong> through the county court (the online Money Claim service for smaller amounts). This is only worthwhile if the tenant can be traced and has money or assets. Take advice first.</li>
<li><strong>A guarantor</strong>, if there was one, depending on the guarantee wording.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Practical tips</h2>
<ul>
<li>Do not start repairs until you have photographed everything and your insurer has had a chance to inspect.</li>
<li>Ask your contractor for <strong>separate line items</strong> for clearance, cleaning, electrics, damp and each repair. It makes both the deposit and insurance claim easier.</li>
<li>Keep <strong>waste transfer notes</strong>. They show the rubbish was real and was disposed of properly.</li>
</ul>
