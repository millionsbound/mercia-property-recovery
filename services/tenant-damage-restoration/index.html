Abandoned belongings: be careful

If a tenant has left items behind, you cannot simply bin them straight away. Write to the former tenant at their last known address and any forwarding address, give them a reasonable deadline to collect, and keep a record. Take legal advice if the items look valuable. This is separate from rubbish and obviously worthless items.

Get the evidence right first

Deposit adjudicators and insurers decide claims on evidence. Before any work starts:

  1. Compare the property against the check-in inventory room by room.
  2. Take dated photos and video of every room and every defect.
  3. Get itemised quotes or invoices for each repair.
  4. Keep copies of emails and letters with the tenant.

The mydeposits case study we cite shows how this works in practice: each item was judged against the check-in and check-out reports, and the award was limited to the amount of the deposit. Our deposit guide explains what to do if the damage costs more than the deposit.

Can the specialist provide the evidence for my deposit claim?

They can provide photos, a written scope and itemised invoices. The check-in and check-out reports still need to come from you or your inventory clerk.

Yes. Hoarding clearances need patience and care, especially if the occupier is still involved. Tell us about the situation when we call.
