f:T907,<h2>First: who is responsible for what</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The meter, the main fuse (cut-out) and the cable coming into the property</strong> belong to the energy supplier and the local distribution network, not to you. In most of the West Midlands that network is National Grid Electricity Distribution. If you suspect tampering, call <strong>105</strong>, the free national number for power cuts and electrical safety, and tell your energy supplier. In the 973-plant Fleet Street case in Birmingham, reported by the BBC, the supply was made safe by the network operator.</li>
<li><strong>Everything after the meter</strong>, the consumer unit and the wiring in the property, is the landlord&#39;s responsibility. That work must be done by a qualified electrician.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What the electrician will do</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Isolate and make safe</strong> any unsafe circuits or connections.</li>
<li><strong>Remove</strong> grow-room wiring, timers, extra sockets and anything that was not part of the original installation.</li>
<li><strong>Inspect and test</strong> the installation and produce an <strong>Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR)</strong> listing what needs fixing.</li>
<li><strong>Carry out remedial work</strong>, from a replacement consumer unit to a partial or full rewire.</li>
<li><strong>Certify</strong> the work. Notifiable work in England must comply with Part P of the Building Regulations, usually by using an electrician registered with a competent person scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Why landlords cannot skip this</h2>
<p>Private landlords in England must have the electrical installation inspected and tested at least every five years under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, and must carry out remedial work identified in the report. After a cannabis farm, the old EICR is worthless. You will need a new one before re-letting.</p>
<h2>Rough cost</h2>
<p>One published estimate puts an electrical rewire and fuse board after a cannabis farm at <strong>£2,500 to £5,000</strong>. A make-safe and test on its own costs less. The electrician will price the remedial work after the EICR. See the <a href="/mercia-property-recovery/guides/cannabis-farm-clean-up-cost/">cost guide</a>.</p>
