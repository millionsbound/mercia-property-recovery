Do the police remove a cannabis farm? What landlords must do next

Yes, partly. The police take the plants, chemicals and growing equipment and make the building safe. They do not clean, repair or hand back a house you can let. Here is what they do, what they leave, and what to do next if police found a cannabis farm in your rental.

The short answer

In the West Midlands, the police do remove a cannabis farm, in the sense of dismantling it. West Midlands Police has a Cannabis Disposal Team of specialist police staff. Its cannabis disposal page says the team's main role is to dismantle cannabis farms safely by removing plants and chemicals, collecting evidence, making the building safe, destroying the equipment used to grow the plants and recycling parts where possible. The plants are burnt at a waste-to-energy plant, overseen by an officer.

What the police do not do is return the property to the state it was in before the grow. Cleaning, the rubbish left behind, the damage, the damp and the electrics are the owner's problem. For a landlord, that is usually where the real cost sits.

What the police do at a cannabis farm

Every case is different, but the reports of West Midlands raids follow a pattern:

  • Entry. Officers execute a warrant or respond to a report. In Kiniths Way, West Bromwich, in 2023, police found up to 250 plants after being called to a reported break-in.
  • Seizure. Plants are seized and, in some cases, kept for forensic examination before they are destroyed. That is what West Midlands Police said about the 183 plants found in Freeman Drive, Sutton Coldfield, in 2022.
  • The supply. Most grows steal electricity. The network operator may need to attend to make the supply safe. In the Middleton Road, Streetly case in 2019, a neighbour told Birmingham Live the "electricity board broke in" that morning.
  • Dismantling. The disposal team takes the set-up apart and removes it so that others can enter safely.
  • Release. The property can stay a crime scene for a while. Nobody should go in until the officer in the case confirms it has been released.

What the police leave for the landlord

Once the keys come back, this is what is usually still there:

  • soil, compost and nutrient spills in carpets and floorboards
  • staples, battens, screws, sheeting and foil fixings on walls and ceilings
  • holes cut for ducting through ceilings, walls and loft hatches
  • a bypassed meter or tampered wiring that needs a qualified repair
  • condensation damage, damp and mould, often with a strong smell
  • the tenant's belongings and ordinary rubbish

West Midlands Police has also warned, in its comments on the Gate Lane, Boldmere case reported by Birmingham Live, that plants grown upstairs can cause floorboards to rot, with a risk of collapse. Treat upper floors with care until someone competent has looked at them.

Police found a cannabis farm in my rental: what to do next

Our first 48 hours checklist has the full detail. In short:

  1. Get the crime reference number and the officer's name, and ask when the property will be released.
  2. Leave the power off. Call 105 about the meter and the supply. Cables can be live even with the consumer unit switched off.
  3. Tell your insurer, today if you can, and ask in writing what they need before work starts.
  4. Photograph everything once you are allowed in: every room, the loft, the meter cupboard and the outside walls.
  5. Check who has possession. If the tenancy has not ended, the tenant still has rights even if they have been arrested. Changing the locks or clearing their things without a court order can be unlawful eviction. GOV.UK explains the current possession rules. Take advice.
  6. Secure the property once you have lawful possession.
  7. Tell your lender and the council's licensing team if your mortgage or licence requires it.
  8. Book the clean-up in the right order: make safe the electrics, clear and decontaminate, dry out, repair, decorate, certify. That is what cannabis farm removal firms do.

Will the landlord get in trouble?

Usually not, if you did not know. Under section 8 of the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971, it is an offence for an occupier or a person concerned in the management of premises to knowingly permit the production of a controlled drug there. The word that matters is knowingly. Records of your referencing, inspections and any concerns you reported are what show you acted properly. If the police ask to interview you, speak to a solicitor first. This is general information, not legal advice.

Where premises have been linked to nuisance or disorder, the police or the council can also use closure powers under the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. A closure notice or order can stop anyone entering for a period, owners included, and the court must hear from people with an interest in the property. If you receive one, take advice quickly.

Who pays for what

Cost Who usually pays
Dismantling the grow and destroying the plants The police
Cleaning, waste removal and odour treatment The owner, or their insurer if the policy covers it
Damage repair, damp and decoration The owner or insurer, with a possible claim against the tenant or deposit
Repairs to the supply before the meter Often billed by the network operator. In one Yardley case it was nearly £3,000
Stolen electricity The supplier may try to recover it. Who is liable depends on the facts and the contract

If officers forced a door or window, ask the officer in the case how the force deals with damage caused while executing a warrant, and tell your insurer. Do not assume either will pay.

The Yardley case, reported by Birmingham Live, is a fair warning. The family said they paid nearly £5,000 in total and could not claim any of it on insurance. Our insurance guide covers why claims fail and our cost guide shows published prices.

Can I ask the police to clear the rest?

No. The disposal team's job is to dismantle the set-up and make it safe for others to enter. Everything after that is a job for the owner. Use a firm that is registered as a waste carrier, gives you a waste transfer note for each load, and photographs the property before it starts. Our cannabis farm removal page explains how to check a firm's registration in two minutes.

What to tell a removal firm when you call

A firm can only quote properly if it knows what it is walking into. Have these ready:

  • the crime reference number and the date the property was released
  • how many rooms, and whether the loft, garage or outbuildings were used
  • whether the meter was bypassed and whether 105 or the network operator has attended
  • your photos, if you have them
  • whether your insurer is involved and wants to approve contractors
  • whether anyone is still living there, or belongings are still inside

Questions landlords ask

Do the police tell the landlord when they find a cannabis farm?

Often, but not always straight away. If you hear about a raid from a neighbour or the news, call 101 with the address and ask for the officer in the case and the crime reference number.

How long until the police release the property?

It varies. Some are released within a day or two, others take longer if the investigation needs more time at the scene. Ask the officer in the case and do not go in until they confirm it.

The police said the building is safe. Can I switch the power back on?

No. Safe for officers to work in is not the same as a safe electrical installation. Call 105 and get a registered electrician to test it before anything is switched on.
